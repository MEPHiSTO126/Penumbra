---
title: 'The Chain and the Shield'
description: 'Hypocrisy as both the mask that conceals us and the restraint that keeps us from worse.'
pubDate: 2026-11-23
---

What would happen if humans stopped hiding their true hearts? Would society still run as usual?

Almost everyone, if not everyone, pretends to be what they are not. Some even lie to themselves that they are somewhat "righteous". Of course, most people will openly admit to not being righteous. Yet, in their hearts, they quietly believe that merely admitting their flaws while committing unrighteous acts (even by their own definitions) makes them superior to those who claim to be righteous and fall short. By branding the latter as hypocrites, they crown themselves the most righteous citizens in a world of unrighteousness. Reality, however, tells a different story. This moral hierarchy exists entirely in their own minds. On the true scale of unrighteousness, the absence of hypocrisy within a vast mountain of wrongdoing does not weigh any less than the presence of hypocrisy within a far smaller pile of faults.

Beyond this self-deception, most humans feed off the moral gratification of performing so-called good deeds. This appears to be an innate part of human nature. Once people perform an act society deems virtuous, someone has to hear about it. It works the other way, too: the moment people are watching, they suddenly find a reason to do good.

I do not consider this genuine goodness. One might argue that the good deed itself outweighs whatever selfish motive produced it. Yet, in reality, the gratification they extract from the act flatters their ego and outweighs the good they have supposedly done.

Consider a stark example. Everyone agrees that pedophiles are wrong and deserve punishment for their behavior. Now, how do we morally gauge the people who go out of their way to track down and beat them? They rarely do this out of a pure pursuit of justice. Rather, they do it for the moral gratification and applause they receive online. Those who cheer this behavior on exhibit the exact same performative impulse, even going so far as to label anyone advocating for due process and actual justice as an accomplice to the crime. The same dynamic drives mobs that beat or stone thieves to death. Meanwhile, everyone commits despicable deeds in secret while convincing themselves their own reasons are justified. This is the height of human hypocrisy.

However, I do not consider this hypocrisy alien to human nature, nor do I think it makes humans inherently bad. Humans simply are as they are. Whatever true empathy (which is, I suppose, a more heartfelt form of sympathy), selflessness, or benevolence a person shows still usually stems from projecting another's misfortune onto oneself. In other words, it is feeling pity for yourself if you were in their shoes. Even the greatest human expression of selflessness remains selfish at its core.

Still, we should not be too quick to judge mankind. Such harsh judgment only arises from placing unrealistic expectations on humans to perform something for which they have no innate capability. In fact, these very expectations, imposed by religion, ethics, and social morals, are what enable the hypocrisy that allows humans to act in a manner otherwise unbefitting of their nature. Without this restraint, the world would devolve into a chaotic hellscape where every individual acts solely for momentary self-benefit. Anyone who believes they are free to inflict evil on others inevitably invites evil upon themselves, creating a cycle where one harms many, and many harm that one in return.

Ultimately, this hypocrisy is a two-sided coin. It shields us from the unfiltered evil of human nature. <em class="closing">Hypocrisy is the chain that confines humanity, and the very thing that keeps it from running wild.</em>
